# My Goals As A Learning Designer Note: This was written in 2024 for a course in my Master's program. Currently in the field of technical writing, I specialize in developing accessible documentation and content experiences. I have extensive experience designing and delivering training–in an unofficial capacity–to technical writers. With each new training I created and facilitated, the more I fell in love with learning design. Today, I’m just over halfway through the M.A. Education program where I am specializing in both Learning Design and Technologies, and in Human Rights and Social Justice. I entered this program because of my desire to help build more equitable and inclusive learning experiences for all learners, and each of my learning design goals can be traced back to that ultimate goal. By the end of this course, I want to deepen my knowledge of conducting learning needs assessments. As a professional technical writer and editor, most of my career experience with learning design has been in an unofficial capacity, coming predominantly from first-hand observations and experiences (e.g. trial-and-error), as well as from transferable technical writing principles. Throughout this program, I have been able to refine the self-taught skills and develop new ones altogether. "Learning Needs Assessments" is one area I have, unsurprisingly, not come across in the field of technical writing, yet it is an essential aspect of learning design. Exceptional learning experiences include learners of all backgrounds, education levels, experiences, identities, abilities/disabilities, etc. Without a needs assessment, how can we know enough about who our learners are and what their needs are? Providing an exceptional learning experience starts with an exceptional plan, and an exceptional plan requires accurate, robust, and relevant data. By building a more solid understanding of how to identify knowledge and skill gaps among diverse learners, I can progress from creating good learning experiences to creating exceptional ones. On a personal level, this goal is also the perfect marriage of my passion for equitable, inclusive education and my innate interest in data and analysis. This course has an entire unit dedicated to needs assessments. Before the end of this course, I plan to apply what I learn by conducting at least one needs assessment at my current job. There are several projects I have on the horizon that would benefit from a needs assessment during the preparation/planning stage. Feedback will be essential for refining my skills, so I plan to inform essential stakeholders of my plan and integrate a feedback loop with them. My mid-term goal is to be proficient in data tools and technologies used by instructional designers. Since I'm already past the mid-way point in my program, I hope to accomplish this goal by January 2025. I learned about data collection and analysis techniques in my research and evaluation course earlier this year. Since then, when I’ve had the opportunity to collect and analyze data at my job, I have conducted the data collection and analysis mostly manually. Luckily, some of my future and upcoming work projects will require qualitative and quantitative data collection and analysis; I aim to use data collection and analysis technologies, including AI tools, to build my proficiency with them. Data collection and analysis is a universal skill, and a skill I have a strong personal interest in. I love making sense of information–whether that information is in the form of words or numbers–which is why my educational and professional background is in technical writing and editing. Much like how instructional design is not a one-size-fits all approach, breaking down complex information into simple, understandable language is not a one-size-fits all approach. Technical writing and learning design both require knowing your audience, measuring impact, and iterating. By reaching this goal, I will be more effective and efficient at both learning design and documentation development. By the end of this program, I plan to have a well-rounded portfolio that demonstrates my expertise in designing equitable, engaging, effective, and accessible learning experiences. Through this portfolio, I hope to start building the credibility necessary to speak at conferences and contribute to the body of knowledge on equity, inclusion, and accessibility for learners who are LGBTQ+ and/or disabled. As someone who was not able to live up to my full academic potential in my youth due to systemic barriers and lack of education on disabilities, I know first-hand what it’s like to move through life feeling inadequate. As a white person who was raised in a middle-class family, I also know the immense impact that sufficient resources can have on one’s life trajectory. I was a collegiate athlete on a partial scholarship with a built-in support network of teammates, tutors, coaches, trainers, etc. Without that privilege, my life could have taken a different path. Such financial, academic, and emotional support shouldn’t be a privilege reserved only for those who can afford it. By sharing my experiences, knowledge, and insights, I hope to help enlighten others on the experiences of LGBTQ+ and disabled learners, inspiring people to take steps towards an educational system that’s equitable and accessible for all. As a lifelong learner with a natural curiosity, I thrive on feedback. To create a robust portfolio as I envision, feedback will be essential: Feedback from professors, classmates, friends, colleagues, and a mentor. Similarly, having a mentor could significantly help me reach my previous goals of becoming proficient with data collection and analysis tools, and deepening my knowledge of needs assessments. At my previous company, a work-friend had offered to mentor me when I mentioned I was starting this program. She is a curriculum developer and a member of the LGBTQ+ community, like myself. Prior to going into curriculum development, she was a high school teacher for several decades. With commonalities in our personal lived experience and with her extensive educational experience, she would be a perfect mentor. With these plans in mind, my goals feel achievable. I look forward to the rest of this course, the rest of this program, and beyond!
