Blinking content: hearing the problem before reading the fix

A remediation guide for Pope Tech, built so readers leave understanding the principle rather than just pasting the patch. The screen reader demo makes the cost audible in about ten seconds.

My role

Wrote the guide and fully produced the screen reader demo video

Audience

Developers and site owners fixing issues flagged by the platform

Format

Remediation guide with a screen reader demo, 2 worked code examples, and scaffolded further reading

Standard

WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A)

The brief

Pope Tech's platform flags accessibility issues on a customer's site. Flagging the issue is the easy part. Getting someone to fix it is harder, because most remediation docs explain what is wrong and skip why it matters.

The goal: write the fix so a reader can apply it in minutes, and frame it so they recognize the same problem the next time it appears in a different form.

Who blinking content affects

  • 01

    Readers who already find static text difficult, for whom animation makes the content harder still.

  • 02

    People with cognitive and neurodevelopmental disabilities, for whom movement can be overwhelming or make it very difficult to focus on the rest of the page.

  • 03

    Screen reader users, who may hear the text re-announced every time it reappears. This is the one people underestimate, and the one the demo makes impossible to ignore.

Key design decisions

The choices that shaped the guide, and why I made them.

Impact before remediation

The guide opens with who this affects and how, before it shows a line of code. Three distinct groups, which teaches that an accessibility issue rarely affects only one population. A reader who understands the cost is more likely to fix it than a reader handed a rule.

Let them hear it

Reading that a screen reader may re-announce the text is abstract. Hearing it loop is not. The demo does in ten seconds what a paragraph cannot, and it is the reason this guide works on people who skim.

Easy fix first

Example 1 is removal, which is the right answer most of the time and takes one deletion. Example 2 handles the case where motion is genuinely required, with a 5-second cutoff and a user control. Most readers are done before the harder example appears.

Error code beside fixed code

Both examples show the broken version and the corrected version together, so a reader can pattern-match against their own codebase instead of parsing prose and translating it back into markup.

Write past the deprecated tag

Most browsers no longer support the blink tag, so a guide written only about it would read as legacy trivia. The principle generalizes: any motion lasting more than 5 seconds needs a pause, stop, or hide control. The guide names auto-playing banners as the current version of the same problem.

Scaffold the next step

Two exits at the end: a deeper piece on designing accessible animation for readers who want practice, and the WCAG 2.2 Understanding doc for readers who need the source. Neither is required to apply the fix.
